# The Heterogeneous Causal Inference for Optimal Extubation Timing in Critical Ill Patients
## BACKGROUND: 
Extensive evidence suggests that both premature extubation and prolonged intubation are associated with extubation failure, accompanied by a significant decrease in patient survival rates. However, there is no consensus within the medical community on the optimal timing of extubation. This study aims to determine the optimal extubation window by quantifying the effects observed in the data.\\
## METHODS: 
This retrospective study was conducted using the latest version of the MIMIC-IV 3.0 electronic health record database. Following standard definitions, patients who experienced reintubation or death within 48 hours after extubation were classified as extubation failures. According to the guidelines of the Society of Thoracic Surgeons, the impact of early and prolonged extubation in different time windows was evaluated using a heterogeneous causal inference framework. Double Machine Learning(DML) and Double Robust(DR) methods formed the core of the framework, with multiple machine learning models compared and selected to ensure the most accurate and unbiased causal estimation. SHAP technique was used to provide risk factors and explainability for the evaluation. A wide range of confounders were considered, including demographics, clinical stability criteria, full blood count, and predictors of the spontaneous breathing trial (SBT). Heterogeneity analysis was performed to account for variations in patient age, ICU types, and comorbidities. The code is available at https://github.com/YYZ-TJU/dml-mimiciv.
## RESULTS: 
The analysis identified 34,148 patients who underwent invasive mechanical ventilation and 46460 patients generated using the synthetic minority sampling technique (SMOTE). In the early extubation strategy, the optimal extubation time was consistently and significantly observed to be within the 4 to 8 hour window in both datasets. In contrast, for prolonged intubation scenarios, the optimal extubation time varied greatly depending on the individual patient's conditions, exhibiting substantial heterogeneity.
## CONCLUSIONS: 
The optimal duration of extension was 4 to 8 hours for early extubation and 24 to 28 hours for prolonged extubation.
